CAMO Annotation Essentials

The short path through Causal Mosaic 0.10.0

The schema has over a hundred slots. A typical finding needs ten of them, plus a handful more when the paper gives the detail. This guide covers only those, in the order you fill them in, using Murphy (2005) as the running example. Murphy transplanted bloodroot (Sanguinaria canadensis) at several densities to suppress garlic mustard (Alliaria petiolata) in an Ontario woodlot.

About this guide

CAMO records what a paper claims causes what. Every claim becomes a small graph. Each node is a state or change in some ecological thing. The edge between two nodes carries how strongly the authors make the claim, what kind of causation it is, and what evidence it rests on.

This page leaves out most of the schema on purpose. Annotate everything it covers and you have a complete, useful record. The full Annotation Guide and the Schema Guide cover the rest: the 16 causal features in depth, fuzzy cognitive map weights, evidence-base assessments and parameter options. Go to them when a paper needs something this page doesn't cover.

About the examples The YAML snippets come from a gold-set extraction of Murphy (2005), trimmed to the fields under discussion. Where the extraction made a mistake this guide warns against, the snippet shows the corrected form. The Common mistakes section shows the originals. Together the snippets validate against causalmosaic.yaml 0.10.0 with linkml-validate.

Three tiers of fields

The quickest way to simplify CAMO is to sort its fields by how often you touch them.

Tier 1 · Always

Every node and edge. Validation fails without the starred ones.

  • Node: name*, entity_type, entity_term*, measured_attribute, state_or_change_qualifier
  • Edge: subject*, predicate*, object*, causal_language*, philosophical_accounts*
  • negated: true for null results

Tier 2 · When the paper says so

Fill these in when the text states them. Otherwise leave them out.

  • life_stage, applied_to
  • dose / dose_level, comparator
  • strength, direction, scope
  • temporal_extent, evidential_basis
  • mediation, context_dependence

Tier 3 · Leave alone

Filled in by Loom, defaulted, or for specialist passes.

  • id, variable_key, provenance, schema_version
  • The other features: necessity, sufficiency, stability …
  • fcm_weight, certainty_grade, parameter_options, composition

Tier 2 has one rule: annotate what the paper says, and nothing it doesn't. An empty field means "not addressed", and an omitted field is read that way, so you never have to type not_addressed.

The smallest valid record

Here is Murphy's headline result with Tier 1 fields only. Loom fills in the graph wrapper (graph_id, schema_version, provenance) and the ids. What you write is the two nodes and the edge.

Initial Sanguinaria canadensis densities of as little as 5 ramets/m² suppressed Alliaria petiolata.Murphy 2005 · finding f1
Murphy f1 · Tier 1 only
nodes:
- id: causal_mosaic:bloodroot_initial_density      # set by Loom
  name: Increased initial Sanguinaria canadensis ramet density
  entity_type: taxon
  entity_term: NCBITaxon:3472                       # Sanguinaria canadensis
  measured_attribute: initial planting density
  state_or_change_qualifier: increased
- id: causal_mosaic:garlic_mustard_performance
  name: Decreased Alliaria petiolata performance
  entity_type: taxon
  entity_term: NCBITaxon:126270                     # Alliaria petiolata
  measured_attribute: performance
  state_or_change_qualifier: decreased
edges:

  predicate: causes
  object: causal_mosaic:garlic_mustard_performance
  causal_language: direct_causal                    # "suppressed": unhedged
  philosophical_accounts: [interventionist, counterfactual]

Read it aloud: increased initial bloodroot density causes decreased garlic mustard performance, stated without hedging, as an intervention result. Everything after this point adds detail to that sentence.

Step 1

Pick One Finding

A finding is one sentence in which the authors claim that something affects something else. Annotate one finding at a time. Each finding gets its own small graph: the Murphy extraction has 25 findings and 72 edges.

What counts as a finding

Murphy sentenceFinding?
"Initial S. canadensis densities of 9 and 11 ramets/m² resulted in the lowest numbers of late-spring A. petiolata seedlings…"✅ Yes. A driver ("densities") changes an outcome ("numbers of seedlings").
"It appears that intermediate and higher densities … deprived A. petiolata of sufficient resources…"✅ Yes, hedged. Record the hedge in causal_language rather than skipping the sentence.
"[Outcomes] … were not affected by initial S. canadensis density."✅ Yes, a null result. Annotate it; don't drop it.
"Leafing phenologies of year 2 A. petiolata and ramets of S. canadensis overlapped."⚠️ Borderline. On its own this is description, not a causal claim. Annotate it only if the authors use it as a cause elsewhere. Murphy does in f16, where bloodroot's leafing timing becomes the mediator.
A methods sentence saying how and when the ramets were transplanted.❌ No. Methods. This belongs in the source document and edge context, not in a finding of its own.

Make the sentence stand alone

Before you annotate, rewrite the finding so it makes sense on its own. Pull the site, the date and the starting conditions in from the surrounding text. Murphy's f1, as extracted:

In a field additive-series experiment in a degraded woodlot near Waterloo, Ontario, as of 2000 (transplanted 1997, with initial Alliaria petiolata densities of 128 seedlings/m² and 31 rosettes/m²), initial Sanguinaria canadensis densities of as little as 5 ramets/m² suppressed Alliaria petiolata, as shown by multivariate repeated-measures ANOVA and simple ANOVAs.Murphy 2005 · finding f1 (standalone form)

Every phrase in that sentence maps to a field you will meet in Step 4: near Waterloo to context_dependence.geographic_scope, as of 2000 (transplanted 1997) to temporal_extent, as little as 5 ramets/m² to dose, ANOVA to strength.analysis_method. A good standalone sentence makes Step 4 a matter of copying.

Stay at the claim level Encode what the authors argue, not their data tables. Murphy tested many densities. You do not make one node per density. Make one driver node ("initial bloodroot density") and record the level on the edge, in dose.
Step 2

Build the Nodes

A node is never just a thing. Bloodroot doesn't cause anything by being bloodroot. A change in some property of bloodroot does. So every node takes the same three parts:

entity_termwhat thing + measured_attributewhat property + state_or_change_qualifierwhat happened to it → "Decreased A. petiolata seedling number"

Add an entity_type and a readable name and you have the five fields every node needs:

FieldWhat to putMurphy example
nameA readable label that combines the three parts. This is what appears in rendered sentences and diagrams.Decreased Alliaria petiolata seedling number
entity_typeOne of five values (below).taxon
entity_termThe thing, as an ontology CURIE if one exists, otherwise free text.NCBITaxon:126270
measured_attributeThe property that changed. A CURIE (PATO and similar) or free text.number of individuals
state_or_change_qualifierWhat happened: increased, decreased, present, absent, introduced, removed, occurred … (full list).decreased
One node per measured outcome Murphy's f2 lists nine outcomes from one driver: seedling numbers, rosette numbers and sizes, stem-leaf numbers and areas, flowering individuals, flowers, siliques, and height. That is one subject node, nine object nodes and nine edges. Don't merge them into a single "garlic mustard performance" node when the paper reports them separately. Reuse a node when the same variable appears again in the same finding.

The five entity types

🌿

taxon

A property of a single taxon: its density, cover, height, mortality. Put the NCBITaxon CURIE in entity_term.

Murphy: almost every node. A. petiolata seedling number, rosette size, height at flowering; S. canadensis initial density.

🌡️

environmental_variable

A measurable state of the environment that is not a property of one taxon: soil pH, light, available resources.

Murphy: "resources available to A. petiolata" (f21), with applied_to naming garlic mustard.

🧩

composite_variable

A state of an aggregate: an assemblage, community or guild. List the members in applied_to.

Murphy: "other native species" in f30, which reports no regeneration of other natives in any density treatment.

🔄

environmental_process

Something that happens over time: competition, leafing, invasion, decomposition. Processes usually take occurred, initiated, increased or decreased.

Murphy: S. canadensis leafing (f16); competition between the two species (f6); invasion by A. petiolata (f23).

🛠️

management_intervention

A deliberate human action. entity_term names the action (planting, burning, removal). What it was done to goes in applied_to.

Murphy: "planting of S. canadensis" (f23): entity_term: planting, applied_to NCBITaxon:3472.

Taxon or intervention? Murphy's driver can be read two ways. When the paper compares densities, the variable is a property of bloodroot: taxon, with measured_attribute: initial planting density. When it talks about the act ("planting intermediate densities … is a good strategy"), it is a management_intervention. Choose by what varies in the claim, and keep the choice consistent within a finding.

Ontology terms, simply

  1. Taxa: always use NCBITaxon. Bloodroot is NCBITaxon:3472, garlic mustard NCBITaxon:126270.
  2. Other entities and attributes: use a CURIE when you find a good one (ENVO, PATO, GO, CHEBI, PO). Use only the prefixes declared in the schema. An undeclared prefix doesn't resolve.
  3. No good term? Write free text ("leafing", "initial planting density"). Free text is valid and gets curated later. A wrong CURIE is worse than honest free text.

Life stage and applied_to

Keep life stage out of measured_attribute. If you write "number of seedlings" for one node and "number of year 2 rosettes" for another, they look like different variables when they are the same variable, number of individuals, at different stages. Put the stage in its own pair of fields:

  • life_stage: one of seed_or_seed_bank, germinant_or_seedling, juvenile, ramet_or_clonal_unit, reproductive_adult, senescent_or_dead, mixed_or_whole_population.
  • life_stage_term: optional detail. A PO development-stage CURIE for plants, or the paper's own words when no term draws the distinction ("year 1 rosette").
Murphy f2 · one of the nine outcome nodes
- name: Decreased Alliaria petiolata rosette number
  entity_type: taxon
  entity_term: NCBITaxon:126270
  measured_attribute: number of individuals    # not "number of rosettes"
  state_or_change_qualifier: decreased
  life_stage: juvenile
  life_stage_term: year 1 and 2 rosettes       # Murphy's own distinction

applied_to names what a node acts on or is measured on, as a list of entity references. Use it for an intervention's target, an aggregate's members, or the organism a process belongs to:

Murphy f16 · a process attached to a taxon
- name: Sanguinaria canadensis leafing
  entity_type: environmental_process
  entity_term: leafing
  measured_attribute: timing
  state_or_change_qualifier: occurred
  applied_to:
  - entity_type: taxon
    entity_term: NCBITaxon:3472
Step 3

Draw the Edge

An edge links a subject node to an object node through a predicate. Five fields make a valid edge: subject, predicate, object, causal_language and philosophical_accounts.

Increased initial bloodroot density causes Decreased garlic mustard seedling number

Predicate: start with four

CAMO has eleven predicates. Most findings need one of these four:

PredicateUse whenMurphy
causesThe authors present the subject as bringing about the change in the object.Density → seedling number (f2)
contributes_toThe subject is one factor among several. Often the honest choice in ecology.Local plantings that "may help" mitigate spread across many locales (f33)
preventsThe subject blocks or holds back the object.Planting bloodroot → spread of garlic mustard (f23)
associated_withA co-occurrence with no causal claim.Low density with weak competitive ability (f10)

The other seven are enables, disrupts, regulates, mediates, moderates, correlated_with (only when a correlation statistic is reported) and precedes. The full guide explains when to use each.

Read it aloud: no double negatives The node says which way things moved. The predicate says how the subject bears on it. "Planting prevents decreased invasion" says the opposite of what Murphy means. Use either prevents → "invasion by A. petiolata" or causes → "decreased invasion by A. petiolata", not both.

Causal language: how firmly is it claimed?

causal_language records the authors' wording, not your opinion of the evidence. Pick a value from the verb and any hedges around it:

no_relationshipExplicitly no effect. Always with negated: true."were not affected by" (f7)
associationalCo-occurrence, no causal verb."usually could not compete" at low density (f10)
uncertain_causalCausal verb, hedged."It appears that … deprived" (f21); "results suggest" (f23)
direct_causalCausal verb, unhedged."suppressed" (f1); "resulted in" (f2)
Two pairing rules (validation enforces them) associated_with, correlated_with and precedes take only associational or no_relationship. causes never takes associational.

Philosophical accounts: what kind of causation?

philosophical_accounts is a list of the ideas of causation the claim relies on. Select every one that fits. Two or three is normal. In restoration papers, five cover nearly everything:

AccountThe cue in the textMurphy
interventionistSomeone manipulated the cause and the effect changed.Density treatments in a planted experiment (most edges)
counterfactualA comparison: with versus without, more versus less."densities above 7 ramets/m² … killed significantly more seedlings" (f16)
mechanisticA how: a pathway, a process, an organized activity.Leafing early enough to kill seedlings (f16); resource deprivation (f21)
probabilistic"usually", "tends to", raised or lowered chances."usually could not compete" (f10)
agencyFramed as a means a manager can use to get a result."a good strategy for managing invasions" (f23)

transmission, regularity and inus_component are rarer. See Conceptions of Causation.

Null results

A finding that something had no effect is evidence. Annotate it. CAMO has exactly one way to encode it:

Alliaria petiolata number of seedlings in early spring … [was] not affected by initial Sanguinaria canadensis density.Murphy 2005 · finding f7
Murphy f7 · null result
nodes:
- name: Alliaria petiolata seedling number
  entity_type: taxon
  entity_term: NCBITaxon:126270
  measured_attribute: number of individuals
  life_stage: germinant_or_seedling
  # no qualifier: the object stays as a positive finding would leave it,
  # and is never "unchanged"
edges:
- subject: causal_mosaic:bloodroot_initial_density
  predicate: causes                 # the relationship that was tested
  object: causal_mosaic:garlic_mustard_early_spring_seedlings
  negated: true                     # always together ...
  causal_language: no_relationship  # ... with this
  philosophical_accounts: [interventionist]
  strength:
    status: explicitly_asserted
    significance: not_significant
  temporal_extent:
    observation_seasons: [early_spring]
The null-result rule negated: true if and only if causal_language: no_relationship. Keep the predicate a positive finding would have used. Never encode "no effect" by giving the object node state_or_change_qualifier: unchanged. That qualifier means the paper reports a stable state as a result.
Step 4

Add What the Paper Says

Everything in this step is optional and comes from the text. Work back through your standalone sentence and fill in a field for each phrase that carries context. Skip a field when the paper is silent on it.

Dose and comparator

dose and dose_level

How much of the driver?

Use dose for a number: numeric_value or minimum_value/maximum_value, a unit CURIE, and the phrase in verbatim. Where there's no unit CURIE (most density units), use unit_text. Use dose_level for a named level with no number: low, intermediate, high.

Murphy f1: "as little as 5 ramets/m²" gives minimum_value: 5, unit_text: ramets/m2. f23: "intermediate densities" gives dose_level: intermediate.

comparator

Compared with what?

A list with one entry per comparison arm. The common comparator_type values are untreated_control, pre_treatment_baseline, reference_undisturbed, alternative_treatment and alternative_dose_level. Set status to explicitly_asserted if the paper names the comparison, or implicitly_assumed if the design implies it.

Murphy: densities are compared with other densities, so comparator_type: alternative_dose_level. In f16 the arm is "7 ramets/m² or fewer", recorded in comparator_dose.

Strength and direction

strength

How big, and was it tested?

At minimum, record significance (significant, not_significant, marginal, not_tested, not_reported) and analysis_method (anova, manova, regression, mixed_model …). Add an effect size, p_value, or dose_response: true with a dose_response_shape when given.

Murphy f1: "multivariate repeated-measures ANOVA" gives significance: significant, analysis_method: manova. f16: an effect only "above 7 ramets/m²" gives dose_response_shape: threshold.

direction

Is it clear which way the arrow points, and why?

status: asserted plus evidence_for_direction. In an experiment, the manipulation sets the direction, so experimental.

Murphy: density was set by the experimenter, so status: asserted, evidence_for_direction: experimental on every experimental edge.

Time, scope and evidence

temporal_extent

When was the effect measured?

observation_seasons takes enum values in 0.10.0 (early_spring, late_spring, summer, autumn, growing_season …). time_since_intervention_months and _text record when after the treatment the measurement was taken.

Murphy f1: "as of 2000 (transplanted 1997)" gives time_since_intervention_months: 36. f2: "late-spring seedlings" gives observation_seasons: [late_spring].

scope and context_dependence

How far does the claim reach?

scope: assertional (this study), contingent (under stated conditions), prototypical (usually) or universal. When conditions are stated, add context_dependence with geographic_scope, temporal_scope and scope_conditions.

Murphy f1 is about these plots, so assertional, with geographic_scope: degraded woodlot near Waterloo, Ontario. f23 generalizes to "ecological restoration", so contingent.

evidential_basis

What evidence backs this edge?

evidence_types (the design: randomized_experiment, observational_cross_sectional, expert_judgment …) and evidence_objects: does it show a correlation, a mechanism, or both?

Murphy f1: evidence_types: [randomized_experiment], evidence_objects: [correlation]. The difference-making is shown, but not how it happens.

Mediation: when the paper gives a "how"

When the authors name the pathway (A affects C through B), make B a node, add the edges that pass through it, and list B in the parent edge's mediation.

Leafing at initial Sanguinaria canadensis densities above 7 ramets/m² was early enough to kill significantly more Alliaria petiolata seedlings before they matured.Murphy 2005 · finding f16
Increased initial bloodroot density causes Bloodroot leafing causes Increased garlic mustard seedling mortality

The parent edge (density → mortality) carries mediation.mediator_node_ids: [leafing node] and a one-line pathway_description. The worked example below shows it in full.

Full worked example: Murphy f16

Here is a single edge with every Tier 2 field the sentence supports. Nothing has been added that the text doesn't state.

Murphy f16 · density → seedling mortality
- subject: causal_mosaic:bloodroot_initial_density
  predicate: causes
  object: causal_mosaic:garlic_mustard_seedling_mortality
  causal_language: direct_causal                  # "was early enough to kill"
  philosophical_accounts: [counterfactual, mechanistic]
  original_sentence: Leafing at initial Sanguinaria canadensis densities
    above 7 ramets/m2 was early enough to kill significantly more
    Alliaria petiolata seedlings before they matured
  dose:
    minimum_value: 7
    unit_text: ramets/m2                          # no UO term for ramets/m2
    verbatim: above 7 ramets/m2
  comparator:
  - status: implicitly_assumed
    comparator_type: alternative_dose_level
    comparator_dose:
      maximum_value: 7
      unit_text: ramets/m2
  mediation:
    status: explicitly_asserted
    mediator_node_ids: [causal_mosaic:bloodroot_leafing]
    pathway_description: leafing was early enough to kill seedlings
      before they matured
  strength:
    status: explicitly_asserted
    significance: significant                     # "significantly more"
    dose_response: true
    dose_response_shape: threshold                # "above 7"
  direction:
    status: asserted
    evidence_for_direction: experimental
  evidential_basis:
    evidence_types: [randomized_experiment]
    evidence_objects: [both]                      # difference AND mechanism

What to leave alone

You can safely skip these on an ordinary pass. Most are filled automatically or have a sensible default.

FieldsWhy you can skip them
id, variable_key, graph_id, schema_version, provenanceLoom generates them.
necessity, sufficiency, temporal_ordering, specificity, stability, determinism, proximate_distal, contributing_sole, reversibility, proportionalityThey default to not_addressed. Fill one in only when the paper says something explicit, such as "is sufficient on its own" or "effects reversed after removal".
certainty_gradeDefaults to not_assessed. It is set in a separate appraisal pass, not during extraction.
fcm_weight, fcm_weight_sourceUsed for fuzzy cognitive maps. Set when the graph is rendered, not when a paper is annotated.
parameter_options, embedding_text, embedding_vectorPopulated from the parameter catalog and the indexing pipeline.
compositionOnly for composite_variable nodes where the paper gives member shares, ranks or dominance. Murphy has none.
moderationUse it only when a third variable changes the strength of an effect without lying on its path. It is rare in a single-study paper.

Every one of these turned up in the Murphy extraction. Each was corrected in the snippets above.

Checklist

  • Each finding is one standalone sentence, with its context pulled in
  • Each node reads as "qualifier + attribute + entity" and has a sensible entity_type
  • Taxa use NCBITaxon CURIEs. Other terms are a declared-prefix CURIE or honest free text.
  • Life stage is in life_stage, not in measured_attribute
  • One object node and one edge per outcome the paper reports separately
  • Each edge reads correctly aloud, with no double negatives
  • causal_language matches the authors' wording and obeys the predicate pairing rules
  • At least one philosophical account per edge, and every one that fits
  • Null results are annotated, using negated: true + no_relationship
  • Tier 2 fields are filled in only where the text supports them
  • Mediator nodes appear both as nodes and in mediation.mediator_node_ids
